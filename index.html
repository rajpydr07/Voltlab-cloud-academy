What this teaches

  • Start request changes the simulated state to RUNNING.
  • Stop request returns the simulated state to STOPPED.
  • Overload trip prevents restarting until reset.
  • Real equipment needs a properly designed control and protection system.
Safety boundary
This simulation is conceptual only. Do not copy it as a real motor wiring design or work on live equipment.

Practical learning worksheets

Lab exercises

These activities are designed for a supervised classroom or a paper-based learning session. Any physical experiment should use a teacher-approved, current-limited extra-low-voltage training kit.

LAB 01 · THEORY

Verify Ohm's law on paper

Calculate current for 3 V, 6 V and 9 V across a hypothetical 1 kΩ resistor. Compare the results and plot voltage against current.

I = V/R
LAB 02 · THEORY

Series and parallel resistance

For 100 Ω and 220 Ω resistors, calculate total resistance in series and in parallel. Explain why the parallel equivalent is lower than the smallest branch resistance.

Rseries = R₁ + R₂
LAB 03 · SIMULATION

Motor control logic

Use the virtual motor panel above. Test start, stop, overload trip and reset. Write down which states permit a start request.

Trip → restart inhibited until reset
LAB 04 · THEORY

Three-phase power

Use 400 V, 8 A and power factor 0.85. Calculate apparent power and real power using the balanced three-phase formula.

S = √3 Vₗ Iₗ
LAB 05 · OBSERVATION

Read a motor nameplate

With a teacher, inspect a disconnected training motor nameplate. Identify rated voltage, current, frequency, output power, speed and duty. Do not open or energize equipment.

Use manufacturer data

Hazard identification

Make a checklist of isolation, verification, barriers, PPE, earthing and supervision requirements for an electrical training area.
